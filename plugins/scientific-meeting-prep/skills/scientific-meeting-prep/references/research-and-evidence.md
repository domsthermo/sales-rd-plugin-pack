# Research and evidence rules

Use the smallest source set that can support the meeting. Evidence quality matters more than volume.

## Source roles

| Source | Use it for | Do not use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Calendar | Meeting identity, date, time, attendees, organizer, location, agenda | Technical conclusions not stated in the invitation |
| Full email | Customer questions, recommendations, commitments, quantities, products, and context | Claims found only in snippets or inferred from recipients |
| CRM | Account, opportunity, contact, ownership, and pipeline truth | Verbatim customer statements without the original source |
| Customer website | Current public company, program, pipeline, and team context | Independent validation of sponsor-reported outcomes |
| Papers and registries | Methods, peer-reviewed findings, study status, and scientific precedent | Proof that the customer's internal workflow matches the paper |
| Patents | Disclosed compositions, methods, examples, and intended applications | Independent replication or regulatory acceptance |
| Official product documentation | Product mechanism, inputs, outputs, compatibility, limits, and intended use | Proof that the product works on an untested customer sample |
| Distributor or third-party page | Discovery only when a primary page cannot be found | Final product specifications when an official source exists |

## Search sequence

1. Read the complete Calendar event.
2. Extract account, attendees, subject, agenda terms, and technical nouns.
3. Search Email by exact subject and attendee.
4. Search topic variants paired with the account or attendee.
5. Fetch the full message bodies and attachments that materially affect the meeting.
6. Search official customer sources and primary science.
7. Search official Thermo Fisher sources for the specific workflow steps and product boundaries.
8. Stop when additional research no longer changes the agenda, questions, fit, or recommended next step.

## Product-fit language

Prefer:

- `Confirmed workflow` when a customer source explicitly identifies the technique.
- `Strong fit if…` when a specific condition must be verified.
- `Conditional option` when the product may work but sample or method details are missing.
- `Reference precedent only` when the product proves a chemistry or workflow concept but does not match the customer's sample.
- `Not the first fit` when a powerful platform is poorly aligned to the stated problem.
- `Related account signal; linkage unconfirmed` for another order or project that may not belong to the same team.

Avoid:

- `will work`, `is compatible`, or `validated` without direct support;
- implying a generic kit is suitable for an intact particle, complex matrix, regulated use, or off-label sample because it works on a simpler reference sample;
- quoting recovery, sensitivity, capacity, throughput, or degree of labeling outside the stated product conditions;
- treating a company claim, patent example, or internal hypothesis as independently verified.

## Missing information

Missing details become meeting questions. Typical high-value unknowns include:

- exact sample or construct identity;
- amount, concentration, volume, and buffer;
- matrix, species, cell type, or tissue;
- upstream preparation and downstream readout;
- current instrument, incumbent method, or outsourced provider;
- target sensitivity, recovery, loading, throughput, or acceptance threshold;
- compatibility constraints and known failure modes;
- timeline, decision owner, and next milestone.

Leave an item blank or mark it unknown rather than guessing.
